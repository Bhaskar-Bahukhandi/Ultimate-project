## Core Fantasy

You are an outsider with administrator rights in a world that runs on code, and the game keeps
asking whether having the power to change things gives you the right to.

Supporting evidence (authored):
- "Capability is not legitimacy" and "Being restored without being asked is a second deletion"
  (`STORY_BIBLE.md` §1).
- "The player should feel, at the end: they chose a kind of authority, it cost something real,
  and the world goes on without a perfect answer" (`STORY_BIBLE.md` §1).
- Tone: "adventurous / melancholic / funny the way frightened people are funny."

## Unique Hook

**Root Access with a political cost.** It's like Silksong-style combat in a Pokémon-spirited
world, *and also* the player can edit the world's code, paid for in corruption, and the whole
story asks who is allowed to authorize change (pillar P10, the Null Court thesis).

What the documents and code establish:
- **Built:** Root Access edits enemy properties at runtime, with caps (gravity ≥ 0.2, speed ≥ 0,
  no boss HP edits; code review C17). Each use adds corruption; corruption at the cap ends the run
  (`scripts/game_manager.gd:732`). A Firewall charm cuts corruption gain by 40%.
- **Designed:** Root Access "starts narrow and widens with each Source Key fragment" and can never
  edit a boss's HP, set gravity to 0, declare a person cured or decide what someone wants
  (`STORY_BIBLE.md` §2). Corruption changes difficulty, dialogue and perception, never the ending
  directly; recovery comes from "rest, grounding and people".

Whether this hook passes the "and also" test with real players is untested. No external playtest
is recorded in the repo.

### Key Dynamics (Emergent player behaviors)

Designed, not yet observed in play:
- Players weigh using Root Access now against corruption later.
- Players play for skill, because Soul is earned only by parries, perfect dodges and kills.
- Players notice that *when* they act changes the story (P7f), and plan routes around deadlines.
- Players replay to reach other ending families and storylines.

## Core Loop

Durations from the template (30 s / 5–15 min / 30–120 min) are not stated in any document, so
none are claimed here.

### Moment-to-Moment
Side-scroll fights: read telegraphs, parry, dash, attack, and decide whether to spend corruption
on a Root Access edit. Between fights, top-down movement and interaction.

### Short-Term
An encounter, a dialogue scene with a choice, or a quest step, often against a signposted
deadline (Chapter 1 north bridge; Chapter 2 medicine convoy).

### Session-Level
`INTENT UNKNOWN`. In the build, the natural unit is a chapter beat ending in a boss or a
choice that sets flags.

### Long-Term Progression
Seven Source Key fragments (all required to reach the Root of Heaven), companions joining,
three story axes resolving across four acts, then the God King and Story+.

### Retention Hooks
- **Curiosity**: what Aethelgard is; the outside world "stays deliberately uncertain" in the base
  game.
- **Investment**: companions and flags that carry from Chapter 1 to Chapter 10.
- **Social**: none (single-player).
- **Mastery**: Hell mode, Hell-only boss phases, Soul earned by skill.

### Anti-Pillars (What This Game Is NOT)

All from confirmed decisions in `DESIGN_PILLARS.md` and `STORY_BIBLE.md`:
- **NOT online**: no online market or multiplayer. It would add servers, anti-cheat and live
  operations, "roughly a second product" (A5).
- **NOT real-money gambling**: in-game currency only, no paid random items (A6).
- **NOT stamina or equipment-load dodges**: rejected from the story source.
- **NOT companion combat commands, and NOT top-down field combat**: combat is side-scroll only.
- **NOT healing items as the main heal**: healing costs MP.
- **NOT a full paper-doll**: it would multiply animation work per armour piece (§12.9).
- **NOT an ending that fixes everything**: Story+ "never says everything is fixed."

## Risks and Open Questions

### Design Risks
- The hook is untested with players; no external playtest is recorded.
- Two never-cut pillars (P6 five modes, P7f WorldClock) are not implemented yet.
- Narrative combinatorial explosion (roadmap §13: High/High). Mitigation already proposed: four
  ending families carry depth; faction and the deleted are modifiers (`STORY_BIBLE.md` §5).

### Technical Risks
From roadmap §13:
- Time-system complexity (High/High).
- Economy exploits and inflation (High/High); the review found an infinite-gold loop.
- Open-world streaming performance (Medium/High).
- Hell mode feeling unfair (Medium/Medium).
- Save breakage across Early Access patches (Medium/High).

Code review (2026-10-04) rated the foundation 3/10; Phase 0 fixes and ContextStack/InputService
work have landed since, but the spine (TransitionService, SaveService, StoryGraph) is not done.

### Market Risks
- The target audience is undocumented, so market risk cannot be assessed.
- Gambling content affects ratings and is restricted in some regions (§13).
- Legal: licences, AI-content disclosure on Steam, title trademark (§13).

### Scope Risks
- Scope creep / never finishing: High likelihood, Fatal impact (§13).
- Burnout from 84 h/week: High likelihood, Fatal impact (§13). The plan budgets 60 h.
- The roadmap's own assumption A1: if the project stays solo, cut the content budget by ~60%.
- Ten pillars, six of them never cut, for a solo developer.
