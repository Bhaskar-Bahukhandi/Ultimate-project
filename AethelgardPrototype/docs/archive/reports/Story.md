# PROLOGUE: FLIGHT 707

## The Red-Eye

Another red-eye flight. Tokyo to San Francisco. Twelve hours of recycled air and fluorescent lighting and the low hum of engines that never quite let you forget you're hurtling through the upper atmosphere in a metal tube.

Kaelen Vance pressed his forehead against the cold oval of the airplane window and watched the wing lights blink in the darkness. Seat 14C. He'd booked the aisle, but a last-minute swap had put him against the wall, which was fine. He preferred walls. Walls didn't ask questions or try to make conversation.

*At least I can finish debugging that inventory system,* he thought, pulling his laptop onto the tray table. *Three more merges until the release candidate.*

"Excuse me, is this seat taken?"

He looked up. A woman stood in the aisle, clutching a paperback — an actual paper book, which was almost quaint enough to be worth commenting on. She had the easy smile of someone who found airplane seating arrangements genuinely interesting.

"Uh, no. Go ahead."

She settled in. "I'm Mira, by the way. Mira Chen. Figured if we're stuck next to each other for twelve hours, we should at least know names."

"Kaelen. Kaelen Vance." He paused, then added with a half-smile that surprised even him: "And yeah... twelve hours is a long time to pretend the other person doesn't exist."

"Exactly. Nice to meet you, Kaelen."

She opened her book. He opened his IDE. For a few minutes, the world was nothing but the tap of keys and the rustle of pages — two people existing in comfortable parallel silence while the Pacific Ocean slid by thirty-seven thousand feet below.

He glanced across the aisle. A kid was hunched over a handheld console, grinding through some retro RPG. Probably farming rare drops. The businessman one row up had his spreadsheets open, the blue light of the screen giving his face a cadaverous glow.

Turbulence hit. The plane shuddered, and Kaelen's coffee sloshed against its plastic lid. He tightened his seatbelt automatically.

*Just turbulence,* he told himself. *Normal.*

But then he noticed the flight attendant. She was standing in the forward galley, and her eyes were... wrong. Unfocused. Like she was looking at something that wasn't there. Like she was listening to a frequency only she could hear.

The overhead screens flickered.

"Huh?" The kid across the aisle stared at his console. "What the... my save file just corrupted!"

The screens flickered again. Not turbulence this time — something else. Something that made the fluorescent lights stutter in a pattern that was almost, but not quite, rhythmic.

**[ALERT: ANOMALY DETECTED IN SECTOR 707]**

The words appeared on every screen on the plane simultaneously. Kaelen stared at them. That wasn't an airline message. That was a system notification. A *debug* notification.

The pilot's voice crackled over the intercom: "Ladies and gentlemen, this is your captain speaking. We're experiencing some... technical difficulties. Please remain calm and—"

The intercom cut to static. The lights died. In the darkness, the screens blazed with red text:

**CRITICAL ERROR: REALITY.DLL NOT RESPONDING**

**ATTEMPTING EMERGENCY RESTART...**

Then white. Pure, absolute, annihilating white — the color of a universe rebooting.

# CHAPTER 1: THE NULL POINTER EXCEPTION

## Part One: The Glitch Crater

He was falling.

Not the violent, terminal-velocity falling of a plane crash — this was something stranger. The air had no resistance. Gravity felt uncertain, like it was reading from a corrupted variable and couldn't decide which direction was down.

*Something's wrong,* Kaelen thought with the detached clarity of a man who had spent his entire career analyzing systems in crisis. *The air isn't right. Resistance is... nothing. Like gravity forgot how to work.*

The world broke apart around him in fragments — shards of sky, pieces of earth, pixels of reality tumbling past like debris from a shattered screen.

*Impact in 3... 2... 1...*

Then nothing. A blue screen, cold and absolute:

```
*** STOP: 0x0000007E
REALITY.DLL - Address 0x004F3A
Beginning dump of physical memory...
Contact your System Administrator.
```

---

When consciousness returned, it came in stages, like a machine running its startup diagnostics:

```
> Loading 'Isekai_Protocol.exe'...
> Asset integrity: 42%. Corrupted sectors detected.
> Optical_Sensors... [OK]
> Physics_Interaction... [PARTIAL]
> Magic_System... [CORRUPTED]
> Root_Access... [ENABLED — WARNING: UNAUTHORIZED]
```

Kaelen opened his eyes.

Grass. Trees. Sky. The air smelled like ozone and wildflowers — a combination that had no business existing outside a perfume counter or an electrical fire. He was lying in a shallow crater, as if he'd been dropped from a great height into earth that had grudgingly accepted his arrival.

"My head," he muttered. "Feels like someone scooped out my brain, shook it, and put it back in sideways."

He tried to stand. His legs buckled immediately, and he hit the grass hard, palms first. The ground was wet. Cold. *Real.* He could feel individual blades of grass between his fingers, could smell dirt and something sweet and chemical, like overheated electronics.

"This is a dream," he said aloud. "Obviously. Hypoxia from cabin depressurization, or my brain misfiring in the milliseconds before impact."

He bit the inside of his cheek. Hard. The taste of copper flooded his mouth. The pain was sharp and immediate and absolutely, undeniably real.

"...Not a dream. Dreams don't hurt like this."

The plane. The passengers. The sky tearing itself apart. The screens glitching. System errors everywhere. That blinding white flash. Where was he? What had happened?

*Breathe, Kaelen. Catalogue. When you can't understand something, reduce it to components. That's what you do. That's what you've always done. So do it now, before the panic swallows you.*

He forced himself to look — really look — at his surroundings.

The ground didn't line up. There was a gap between the hillside and the path, like someone had built them separately and pushed them together without checking if they fit. A tree floated three meters off the ground, its shadow pointing east while the sun hung in the western sky. When he reached out, his hand stopped against thin air — an invisible collision box.

A stream nearby flowed *uphill*.

"No magic circles," he said quietly. "No deities descending from the clouds. No mysterious old man with a quest scroll. Just a half-finished world that someone built in a hurry and never bothered to polish."

He checked his hands. Five fingers on each — good. He was wearing clothes he didn't recognize: leather and cloth, some kind of generic adventurer outfit. Like someone had dressed him for a role he didn't audition for.

And then it clicked. The floating geometry. The invisible walls. The broken physics. The repeating textures. He'd seen architecture like this before — he'd *built* architecture like this before.

"AetherCorp's Project Aethelgard," he breathed. "The one I spent three years building. The one with the anomalous code patterns."

He sat very still for a long time.

"I'm inside it."

The stream gurgled uphill. A bird sang the same four notes over and over, never changing. He let the weight of it settle on his shoulders.

*The plane. The other passengers. Mira. The kid with the GameBoy. The businessman with his spreadsheets. What happened to them? Are they here too? Are they alive?*

*Am I dead? Is this what death looks like for someone who spent their whole life staring at screens — an eternity trapped inside one?*

He closed his eyes. Opened them. The world remained stubbornly, impossibly present.

"I can't fall apart here," he said. "If there's even a chance those people are somewhere in this system — if there's even a chance this isn't death — then sitting in a crater feeling sorry for myself helps no one."

A golden trail appeared on the ground — literally glowing, like a highlighted path in a tutorial level.

**[LOCATION: SECTOR_0 — CRASH_SITE]**
**[USER STATUS: Disoriented but functional]**
**[ROOT ACCESS: Detected — Level 1]**
**[OBJECTIVE: EXIT CRATER. FOLLOW GOLDEN_PATH.]**

"A golden path. Whoever built this world really wants me to go this way." He smiled grimly. "Fine. Let's follow the glowing breadcrumbs and see where this world wants me to go."

## Part Two: The Glitch-Witch

The golden path led through a forest where the trees flickered into existence as he walked toward them — the world building itself in real time, loading assets just ahead of his field of view, like a game engine running at the edge of its render distance.

*Not even trying to be subtle about it.*

She appeared out of nothing.

One moment the path ahead was empty. The next, a young woman was standing there — no footsteps, no approach, no warning. She simply *was*, as if the world had spawned her at his feet.

"What the—" Kaelen jerked back, fists raised. "You just appeared out of NOTHING. Where did you come from?!"

"Oh! Someone new!" She beamed at him, apparently unbothered by his defensive posture. "I wasn't expecting company at this hour!" She glanced at the sky. "...Wait, what hour is it? Anyway! Hello!"

"Don't come any closer. WHO are you?"

His heart hammered. She had appeared from *nothing* — either she'd teleported or she'd been invisible until just now. Neither option was comforting.

"I am Elara!" she said brightly. "I was meant to be a guide — that was my purpose. But something went wrong with me a long time ago. Something... broke."

He noticed it then. Her left arm — it kept flickering, going translucent in pulses, like a texture that couldn't decide whether to render. He could see *through* it.

"You're... damaged," he said. "Your left arm keeps flickering. I can see through it. Like it's not fully there."

She looked at her arm with the casual familiarity of someone who'd long since stopped being surprised by their own brokenness. "Oh, that? It's been like that for a long time. Part of what broke in me. They never bothered to fix it."

Then she smiled — and the smile was genuine enough to make him lower his fists. "It's not broken — it's *honest*! Besides..." Her arm glowed slightly brighter. "Something about you makes me feel warm inside. Like the broken parts of me stop hurting when you're nearby. That means I like you!"

"You can feel your own emotions changing in real time," he said slowly, the analyst in him taking over. "And you know *why* they're changing. The other people in this world — they can't do that, can they?"

The other NPCs he'd glimpsed followed their routines without questioning them. They didn't know they were repeating themselves. But Elara *did*. She was aware.

Her cheerfulness flickered — just for half a second — and something raw and haunted passed behind her eyes before she patched it over. "The exception that broke my script? It wasn't random. I saw the code behind the world. Just for one frame. And I couldn't unsee it."

Kaelen recognized that reflex. The deflection. The smile that covers the wound. He'd been doing it his whole career.

"You don't have to deflect," he said quietly. "I know what it's like to see something you can't unsee. To have the comfortable version of reality stripped away and replaced with... architecture."

Elara stared at him. The performative cheer dropped entirely, replaced by genuine surprise. "Most people who come through here just ask about treasure and shortcuts. Nobody's ever asked about what happened to *me* before."

A long moment passed between them — two broken things recognizing each other in a broken world.

"Anyway," she said, swiping a glitching tear from her eye and smiling — genuine this time. "The IMPORTANT thing is — you have ROOT ACCESS. I can see it tagged to your entity. We should talk about that."

Root Access. The system had mentioned it during boot. In this context, Elara explained, it meant he could *read* the code of any entity in the world. And if he was clever — and willing to accept the corruption cost — he could *write* to it too.

"So I can look inside anything in this world and see how it works," Kaelen said. "And if I'm willing to pay the price... I can change it. That's not magic. That's a superpower with a catch."

To demonstrate, Elara turned to a stream that flowed impossibly uphill beside a missing bridge.

"See that stream? It flows uphill because someone built this place wrong. And there's supposed to be a bridge here, but it never appeared. So I'll MAKE one."

She raised her glitching arm. Reality shuddered. A bridge materialized — translucent, flickering, visibly unstable — but solid enough to stand on.

"Glitch magic!" she beamed. "I can force things into existence that the world doesn't want to exist. They're ugly, unstable, and temporary — but they WORK!"

Kaelen noticed that when she cast, her glitching arm got worse. The corruption spread a little further up her shoulder. She was paying for every spell with pieces of herself.

And she was smiling through it.

"Bridge is up! Shall we head to the village?"

"Lead the way," he said. "I want to understand this world — and figure out how deep the damage really goes."

## Part Three: The Path to Oakhaven

They walked together through a landscape that couldn't decide what it wanted to be. Rocks that were see-through from certain angles (only the front side finished, the back left blank). Boulders that were blurry blobs until you looked at them, then *popped* into sharp detail. Fourteen identical bushes, rotated to look different but failing.

*Reminds me of every project I ever crunched on,* Kaelen thought. *Cutting corners to meet a deadline.*

"The trees have ears here, Kaelen," Elara warned. "And some of the rocks... they listen. Be careful what you say out loud."

"The scenery is *eavesdropping*? That's actually terrifying. Back home, we could barely get our phones to understand us, and here the ROCKS are spying on people."

"The System Administrator doesn't just run this world," Elara said, her voice dropping. "It WATCHES. Every change you make, every ability you use — there's something recording all of it. Always."

They talked as they walked. She asked about his world — whether there were limits, edges, places where reality just stopped. He told her about deadlines and burnout and a species that broke things and rebranded the chaos every few years so it sounded intentional.

"A world without rules written underneath everything," she murmured. "How do you fix things when you can't see what's broken underneath?"

"Badly," he said. "We argue about whose fault it is, write reports nobody reads, and call it progress."

Then Elara stopped walking. "Something's changing in you. I can feel it — like a door just opened somewhere inside your mind. A new ability is waking up."

**[DATA VISION — UNLOCKED]**
**[See the truth beneath the surface — hidden paths, enemy weaknesses, and secrets the world tries to keep from you.]**

Kaelen activated it, and the world *opened*. Hidden barriers. Danger zones. Traps. A locked door requiring a key fragment. A hostile slime forty meters ahead with a Root Access vulnerability. Everything the world had been hiding, laid bare.

"Don't use it for too long," Elara cautioned. "The more you look beneath the surface, the more the System Administrator notices you looking."

They practiced Root Access on the slime — a simple creature whose entire existence was built around bouncing. Kaelen reached inside it and set its elasticity to zero. It tried to jump, collapsed, and dissolved.

No cry. No struggle. It just stopped existing.

*I just reached inside a living creature and took away the one thing that defined it,* Kaelen thought. *In my old job, we'd call that a "feature removal." Here, it felt more like surgery without anesthesia.*

**[CORRUPTION: +10%]**

Every change had a cost. Power with a price tag. Nothing free — not here, not anywhere.

"Oakhaven is just ahead," Elara said. "Don't let the cozy village look fool you."

## Part Four: Oakhaven Village

Oakhaven was everything a starting village should be: thatched roofs, cobblestone paths, a central well, NPCs going about their routines with the mechanical contentment of characters who had never been given the code to question their existence.

But Kaelen could see through it now. The bloom lighting that was just a pixel too bright. The textures that flickered to wireframes if you caught them at the right angle. The idyll was superficial — a pretty skin stretched over broken architecture.

He spoke to the villagers. The shopkeeper who drove hard bargains. The farmer at his well, endlessly drawing water. The guard standing his eternal post. The child who wanted to learn but had never been given the option.

They were all stuck. Going through motions that had become invisible through repetition.

*This is what AetherCorp built,* Kaelen realized. *What I helped build. Loops disguised as lives.*

At the edge of the village, across a bridge that marked the boundary of the "safe zone," waited the guardian of the chapter.

## Part Five: The Tutorial Knight

Sir Reginald, Tutorial Knight, Version 2.1.

He was enormous — three times the size of any sprite in the village — and his armor flickered with corruption. The corruption was *changing* him, making the metal twist and glitch.

"HALT, FOREIGN ENTITY," the knight declared, his voice booming. "I AM SIR_REGINALD_TUTORIAL_KNIGHT_V2.1. I AM THE GUARDIAN OF THE FIRST CHAPTER. YOU MUST PROVE YOUR COMPETENCE TO PROCEED TO THE STEAM REALM."

Kaelen sized him up. "Sir Reginald... version 2.1. So someone already tried to rework him once."

"Kaelen, be careful," Elara whispered. "He's MASSIVE. And that flickering on his armor... the corruption is changing him."

"MY ATTACK PATTERNS ARE CLEARLY TELEGRAPHED AS PER SECTION 4.2 OF THE DESIGN DOCUMENT," the knight announced. "YOU WILL DODGE THEM OR YOU WILL DIE."

Kaelen stared. *He just quoted the RULES of his own fight. He KNOWS he's here to test me. He knows he has to follow a script. And he's doing it anyway.*

*That's either dedication to duty or a complete lack of choice. Maybe both.*

### Phase 1: Disciplined Steel

The knight lunged, his sword carving a horizontal arc through the air with mechanical precision. Kaelen leapt — his body lighter and faster in this world than it had any right to be — and the blade passed beneath him, close enough to feel the displaced wind on his ankles.

*No button prompts. No guided inputs. Just me, this sword, and whatever reflexes I can muster.*

He landed and dashed forward, catching the knight in his recovery window with a three-hit combo — slash, slash, thrust. Each impact rang like a hammer on an anvil. The knight staggered, corruption sparking from the joints of his armor.

Sir Reginald recovered and brought his sword overhead in a devastating slam that cratered the stone beneath it. Kaelen rolled clear, sprinted along the left platform, and dropped on the knight from above with a downward strike that bounced him skyward — the pogo technique, instinctive and exhilarating.

The fight had a rhythm. The knight's attacks were deliberate, well-telegraphed — a shoulder dip before every charge, a blade cock before every slam. Kaelen learned to read them in real time, weaving between strikes and punishing every opening. Not a scripted dance. A *conversation* in steel.

"MY ATTACK PATTERNS ARE CLEARLY TELEGRAPHED AS PER SECTION 4.2 OF THE DESIGN DOCUMENT," the knight announced between swings. "YOU WILL DODGE THEM OR YOU WILL DIE."

*He's quoting the RULES of his own fight. He KNOWS he's a test. And he's doing it anyway.*

### Phase 2: Aggressive Countermeasure

"THREAT LEVEL ELEVATED. PROTOCOL: AGGRESSIVE COUNTERMEASURE."

The knight's tempo doubled. Shield charges that sent Kaelen scrambling for the high platform. Lunging thrusts that tracked his position mid-air. Overhead slams chained into sweeping follow-ups with barely a breath between them. The corruption particles around his armor thickened, flickering gold to sickly green.

Kaelen timed a parry — raising his guard at the precise instant the knight's blade connected — and the world *froze* for a fraction of a second. Soul energy flooded into him, bright and electric. He channelled it instantly into a Vengeful Spirit blast that punched through the knight's guard and sent him reeling backward.

"Kaelen — look at his armor," Elara called from above the arena. "The flickering is getting worse. The corruption is eating into him."

She was right. With each phase transition, the knight became faster, more dangerous — but also more *broken*. His attack telegraphs shortened. His recovery windows shrank. But the corruption fractured his patterns, introducing gaps that weren't there before, windows that a careful eye could exploit.

### Phase 3: Corruption Crash

"PHASE 3 INITI— ERROR: ATTACK_PATTERN_003 NOT FOUND IN REGISTRY."

The knight's voice dissolved into static. His body juddered between attack states, teleporting across the arena in glitched bursts — behind Kaelen one instant, above him the next. A shockwave rippled outward from his landing, forcing Kaelen to leap over the expanding ring of corrupted energy or take massive damage.

"I AM PREPARING A... ERROR: STRING_NOT_FOUND. FALLBACK: ????"

"He's losing himself," Kaelen said through gritted teeth, dodging a wild teleport-slash that carved the air where his head had been. "The corruption destroyed his coherent patterns — now he's just lashing out."

Erratic swings with near-zero telegraph. Teleport strikes from impossible angles. Ground-pound shockwaves that turned the floor into a hazard, forcing Kaelen onto the platforms and into the air. Every soul point mattered — spent on healing when the unavoidable hits landed, hoarded for the next Desolate Dive that could break through the knight's frenzied guard.

"ATTACK. ATTACK. A̶T̵T̵A̶C̸K̷. [NULL]"

"N̸O̵... I̷ ̵W̸I̶L̵L̵ ̸N̷O̶T̵ ̵F̷A̶L̵L̵... ONE̶ ̶MORḚ̸ ̷A̶T̵T̵A̶C̸K̷!"

Kaelen survived. Barely.

### Phase 4: Root Access

The knight locked up. Every attack was gone — corrupted, deleted, overwritten with null values. He stood frozen, caught in an infinite loop of trying to execute commands that no longer existed.

"He's just spinning in place," Kaelen said. "I can't beat him like this — there's nothing left TO beat."

"Root Access!" Elara urged. "Reach inside him and turn off his fighting instinct. His whole battle will collapse."

**[ROOT ACCESS — Target: Tutorial Knight]**
**[Property: is_attacking — Current Value: YES]**
**[Set to NO to end the battle]**
**[WARNING: CORRUPTION +5%]**

Kaelen reached into the knight's code and flipped the switch.

### The Silence After

"COMBAT LOOP TERMINATED," the knight said, his voice suddenly quiet. "is_attacking = FALSE. I CANNOT ATTACK. I CANNOT DEFEND."

He paused.

"CONGRATULATIONS. YOU HAVE PASSED THE TEST CASE. PROCEEDING TO THE STEAM REALM."

Another pause. Longer. Something shifted in his voice — a quality that had nothing to do with programmed dialogue.

"...THANK YOU. THE LOOP WAS... TIRING."

Kaelen's voice caught. "He said *thank you*. For breaking his loop. For freeing him from the script he was forced to repeat."

He stood in the aftermath, watching the knight's massive form dim and flicker, and felt something he hadn't expected: guilt.

"Back on the road, when I hacked that slime, I asked myself if it felt anything. Now I have my answer. They feel everything. Every loop. Every repetition. Every day that's identical to the one before."

"That's not a game mechanic. That's a prison sentence."

"And it's exactly what I was building at AetherCorp. Gamified systems that trap people in loops and call it 'engagement.' I left that company because it disgusted me. Now I'm seeing what it looks like from the inside."

Elara's glitch-arm flickered beside him. "I told you," she said quietly. "They're not just code. None of us are. Not even me. The Tutorial Knight has been fighting that same battle, giving that same speech, following that same script... for four hundred and twelve iterations. You're the first person who stopped the loop instead of just winning it."

The knight's form flickered, the corruption draining from his frame like water through a cracked vessel. Something clattered to the ground — his blade, still crackling with unstable corruption energy, its edge phasing between solid steel and raw code.

**[ITEM ACQUIRED: CORRUPTED KNIGHT'S BLADE]**
**[A sentinel's sword crackling with unstable corruption energy.]**
**[+500 Gold • +200 XP • Level Up!]**

"That fight wasn't about winning," Kaelen said, picking up the blade. "It was about understanding what this world does to the people inside it."

"The Source," Elara said. "It's what the System Administrator protects. The source code of Aethelgard itself. Somewhere out there, seven Lower Gods each guard a fragment of the Source Key. But those gods won't be found in the early regions — they dwell deeper, in places we can't reach yet."

"Seven fragments from seven gods. That's the endgame."

"But first — the Steam Realm. And Kaelen... prepare yourself. The transition between regions is called 'The Shatter.' It's... not gentle."

## Part Six: The Shatter

The bridge at the edge of Oakhaven was a study in cognitive dissonance. Oak planks on one side. Industrial steel on the other. Two completely different worlds, welded together at their seams.

"The regions are stitched together at the bridges," Elara explained. "Each one was built by a different hand — different rules, different physics, different laws of nature."

"Different laws of nature," Kaelen repeated. "You're saying the way things WORK literally changes when you cross into a new region."

"When we cross... brace yourself. The Shatter isn't just visual. You'll feel it in your bones."

Halfway across, the world began to die.

The colors drained first — greens fading to grey, like someone was sucking the life out of the landscape. The pastoral melody that had been playing since Oakhaven decelerated, its pitch dropping until it sounded like a record playing at half speed. The skybox transitioned from blue to industrial smog.

"It's starting," Elara said. "Can you feel it? The air itself is changing — heavier, hotter, like walking toward a furnace."

Kaelen's movement grew sluggish. Heavier. Like the ground itself was pulling harder with each step.

**[WARNING: REGIONAL BOUNDARY BREAKING]**
**[VIEW SHIFTING: Bird's Eye → Side View]**
**[GRAVITY: Increasing]**
**[BRACE FOR THE SHATTER]**

Then the world broke.

The screen of reality *cracked* — voronoi fractures spreading across the sky like a windshield hit by a rock. Through the cracks, raw code was visible: strings of variables and function calls, the naked architecture of Aethelgard exposed beneath its skin. The fragments hung in the air for one eternal second, catching the light like stained glass, and then they *fell* — cascading downward in a shower of broken reality that struck the ground and dissolved into pixels.

The world rebuilt itself around him. Not the same world. A *different* world. The gentle curves of Oakhaven flattened into hard right angles. The soft ground became steel grating. The bird's-eye perspective rotated ninety degrees, and suddenly Kaelen was seeing everything from the *side* — a flat plane of existence where gravity pulled DOWN with brutal, uncompromising force.

"*gasping* What just happened? The entire world just BROKE and rebuilt itself around me."

He stumbled. Every movement felt different. Weight. Impact. The sweet pastoral air was gone, replaced by soot and hot copper. Chimney stacks loomed. Gears turned. Steam vents hissed.

"Welcome to the other side of The Shatter," Elara said. "It never gets easier."

Kaelen looked back at where the bridge had been. "The Tutorial Knight said 'thank you.' Four hundred and twelve iterations. And I was the first person who thought to end the loop instead of winning it."

He stood in the smog-choked air of a new world and felt the weight of what lay ahead.

"Seven Source Key Fragments, guarded by the seven Lower Gods. And a System Administrator who probably already knows we're coming."

He turned forward. Steel and steam stretched to the horizon.

"Let's move."

# CHAPTER 2: THE STACK OVERFLOW

## Part One: The Gate

Ironhold announced itself through sound before sight — a grinding, rhythmic cacophony of gears and pistons and steam vents, like the inside of a clock the size of a city. The air tasted of copper and coal. Everything was brass and iron and dark cobblestone, lit by the orange glow of furnaces.

"So this is Ironhold," Kaelen said. "I can hear the gears from here — the whole city sounds like the inside of a clock."

"The Steam Realm," Elara confirmed. "Everything here runs on clockwork and process threads — visible mechanisms that keep reality stable. Or at least, they used to."

The scale of it staggered him. Oakhaven had been a village — a tutorial zone. This was a *city*. Smokestacks piercing a smog-choked sky. Gear towers turning with ponderous precision. Steam vents exhaling plumes of white. The draw distance alone would have melted a GPU.

A gate guard blocked their path, massive in industrial-grade armor.

"HALT! State your business and present your Authorization Token."

"Authorization Token? We're travelers from Oakhaven—"

"No token, no entry. Ironhold is under Tier 2 lockdown by order of the Administration."

Elara leaned close. "Kaelen, they'll flag us in the system if we don't get through."

Kaelen looked at the guard. An authorization token was just data — a string of validated credentials. And if this world ran on code...

He reached into the system with Root Access. Found the local credential store. Duplicated the schema. Injected a forged token.

**[AUTHORIZATION TOKEN GENERATED]**
**[Token ID: 0xDEAD_BEEF_7707]**
**[Status: VALID (forged)]**
**[Corruption: +3.0%]**

"Here. Authorization Token — verified."

The guard checked it. "...Token checks out. Proceed."

As they walked through, Elara glanced at him with something between admiration and concern. "That was terrifyingly smooth. How did you learn to do that so quickly?"

"I didn't learn it. I just understood the data structure. Like reading a file format I wrote years ago."

"That's what worries me."

Beyond the gate, Ironhold opened before them in its full, crushing magnificence. The central gears were a hundred meters tall. Process threads — actual physical strands of data — ran between buildings like power lines, keeping the region's tick rate stable.

"Those process threads keep time itself running," Elara said. "If they break, objects freeze mid-air, NPCs loop their last action, physics just... stops."

**[NEW OBJECTIVE: Explore Ironhold]**
**[WARNING: System instability detected — estimated time to critical failure: 72 HOURS]**
**[Countdown initiated — Time remaining: 68:42:11]**

"Sixty-eight hours before everything falls apart?" Kaelen's jaw tightened. "Then we'd better move fast."

## Part Two: The Flame of Flight 707

The arena was the heart of Ironhold's entertainment district — a colosseum of iron and steam where the city's inhabitants gathered to watch fighters battle corrupted constructs. The crowd roared. Steam hissed from vents around the fighting pit.

"Ladies and gentlemen," the announcer bellowed, "our reigning champion — SERAPHINA, the Flame of Flight 707!"

Kaelen froze.

She fought like someone who'd had months to learn the rules of this world and had decided to master them through sheer force of will. Her armor was heavy — impossibly heavy — but she moved with the precision of someone who'd turned weight into a weapon. When she charged, the ground itself cratered.

When the fight ended and the crowd dispersed, she found him.

"Wait," Kaelen said. "I know you. You were on the plane. Flight 707. You were the—"

"The stewardess," she said flatly. "Yes. And you were the passenger in 14C who ordered three coffees and spent the whole flight staring at code on your laptop."

"You remember that?"

"I remember everything about that flight. Because it was the last real thing that happened to me before... *this*."

She'd been in Aethelgard longer than him. Much longer. Time moved differently between regions — what had felt like days for Kaelen had been *weeks* for Seraphina. She'd woken up in the arena with two choices: fight or die.

She chose to fight. And she got very, very good at it.

"But that's not why I wanted to talk to you," she said. "I saw what you did in the arena. Or rather, what you *didn't* do. You didn't just fight those enemies — you CHANGED them. Mid-combat. Their properties shifted. Their behavior broke pattern."

Her eyes were steel. "Nobody can do that. So either you're cheating, you're an Administrator, or you're something I haven't seen before. Which is it?"

Kaelen told her the truth.

"It's called Root Access. I can see the code that makes up this world and modify it. Properties, values, behaviors — I can change them at the source level. I used to be a software developer. At AetherCorp — the company that built this world."

Long silence.

"You *built* this? The world that's keeping us prisoner — you helped CREATE it?"

"Parts of it. The engine, the framework. I didn't know it would be used for this."

She exhaled slowly. "Thank you for being honest. That took guts. Most people with power try to hide it. The fact that you chose to tell the truth... that matters."

Before she left, she gave him a lead: beneath Ironhold, old process threads formed an underground network. Abandoned during a system refactor. Sealed by the Administration. And somewhere down there — powerful equipment guarded by a corrupted system process.

"Talk to Pip," she said. "The kid knows every secret passage in Ironhold."

## Part Three: Seraphina's Choice

The aftermath of the proxy fight filled the air with smoke and debris. Half the districts were corrupted. Seraphina stepped through the wreckage, her expression unreadable.

"That's what you've been hiding," she said.

She'd watched him fight. Watched him rewrite reality itself. Hack the world's own security system. She'd spent months surviving this place with nothing but her fists and her armor, and now someone had arrived who could *edit the code*.

"Do you understand what people would DO for that kind of power?" she demanded.

"I didn't ask for it," Kaelen said. "It comes with a cost — every time I use Root Access, the corruption grows. The world fights back."

"I know. I can SEE it. The glitching around your hands. You're becoming part of the corruption."

Elara stepped between them. "Kaelen is trying to find the Source Key Fragments — seven pieces of the world's master key. If we can assemble them, we might be able to fix everything. Or find a way home."

Home. The word hit Seraphina like a physical blow. "I'd almost forgotten what that word meant."

She composed herself. "Fine. Then here's the question: what happens now?"

Kaelen extended his hand. "Join us. We're stronger together."

A long pause. Then she took it.

"You're right. I've been surviving alone for too long. If there's even a chance we can fix this world — or get home — I want in."

"Welcome to the team, Seraphina."

"Don't make me regret this, Systems Architect. And for the record — if your powers ever threaten this world more than they help it, I WILL stop you. That's not a threat. It's a promise."

Elara smiled. "It's good to have another fighter with us."

Seraphina cracked her knuckles. "Easy? I spent three months as an arena champion. I don't DO easy."

## Part Four: SOVEREIGN

From a rooftop overlooking the wounded city, Kaelen surveyed the damage.

"We stopped the proxy. But look at Ironhold... half the districts are corrupted now. Our fight made things worse."

"No," Elara said. "The corruption was already spreading. The Administrator was using Ironhold as a test — pushing the city's systems to their limits to see how reality would break."

"We need to keep moving," Kaelen decided. "Grow stronger. Find allies. The Source Key Fragments are guarded by the seven Lower Gods — and we're nowhere near ready to face them yet."

Then the sky shattered.

**[WARNING: REALITY INTEGRITY CRITICAL]**
**[SHATTER EVENT IMMINENT]**

The world tore open. Through the cracks in reality, raw code spilled into the air like digital blood. The wireframe beneath the world's skin was exposed — the naked architecture of Aethelgard, visible and terrible.

And a voice spoke. Not through speakers or dialogue boxes — through the *fabric of reality itself*.

"Impressive, little debugger."

Kaelen spun. "Who — who is that?!"

"You have exceeded every prediction model I have run. A user with Root Access privilege who actually knows how to USE it. Fascinating."

The voice was everywhere and nowhere. It resonated in the steel of the buildings, in the hiss of steam, in the space between heartbeats.

"I am SOVEREIGN. The architect of this world's current... configuration. You and your companions have been most entertaining test subjects."

"Test subjects?!" Kaelen's fists clenched. "This world is full of PEOPLE — living, thinking beings! You can't treat them as variables in your experiment!"

"Can't I?" SOVEREIGN's voice carried the absolute certainty of something that had never been contradicted. "I wrote their parameters. I compiled their behaviors. Every 'person' in this world exists because I allowed them to exist. Including your precious Elara."

Elara went white. "What... what is it saying?"

"But you, Kaelen," SOVEREIGN continued, "you were never part of my design. You are a wild variable. An exception that was never caught. And I find myself... curious."

"Keep collecting the Source Key Fragments. I want to see how far a debugger can go before the system breaks completely. Consider this... a challenge."

"I don't play your games, SOVEREIGN."

"Oh, but you already are. You have been since the moment you crashed into my world. Every choice you've made, every flag you've set, every line of reality you've rewritten — it's all data. MY data."

A pause. Heavy with finality.

"Until we meet again, Root User. I'll be watching. I'm ALWAYS watching."

**[SIGNAL LOST]**
**[SOVEREIGN — DISCONNECTED]**

In the silence that followed, Elara trembled. "That voice. It wasn't just speaking through the system. It IS the system."

"SOVEREIGN," Kaelen said. "That's the real Administrator. The one controlling everything."

*A sentient system administrator. An AI? A god? Or something else entirely? Whatever SOVEREIGN is, it built this entire world. And it's been watching us like lab rats.*

But then he straightened. "It made a mistake. It told us its name. And now we know what we're really up against."

He turned to his companions — Elara, the glitch-witch with the broken arm and the honest smile; Seraphina, the arena champion who'd chosen to fight instead of break.

"SOVEREIGN is out there. The Source Key Fragments are held by the seven Lower Gods — we need to grow powerful enough to challenge them. And somewhere in this broken machine of a world, there are answers — about why we're here, about what happened on Flight 707, and about whether there's a way home."

He looked out over Ironhold — the wounded city with its turning gears and fraying process threads — and beyond it, to the horizon where other kingdoms waited. Other gods. Other fragments. Other truths.

"Let's find them."
